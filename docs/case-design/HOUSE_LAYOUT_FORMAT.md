# House layout blockout (no art first)

Written 2026-10-26 by Claude. Decision (user, same day): build every map as a plain blockout first, then paint
backgrounds to match, so doors and walkable floor never have to be adjusted to fit art.

Files: `assets/maps/house-01.layout.json` (data, generated by `tools/case_design/build_house_layout.py`),
`tools/case_design/layout.py` (load, `problems()`, `step_costs()`), `tools/case_design/layout_svg.py`
(review page: `docs/case-design/house-01-blockout/index.html`). Tests: `tests/case_design/test_layout.py`.

## Format

A layout is a graph of **screens** (480x360 logical pixels, doubled to 960x720 in game), the same shape as
`assets/mansion/fixed-mansion.json` so one loader can read both. Per screen:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `id`, `name`, `role` | `room`, `hall` or `secret` |
| `walk_regions` | `[x, y, w, h]` rectangles of open floor |
| `obstacles` | `[x, y, w, h]` furniture footprints (not walkable) |
| `portals` | `id`, `at` (trigger point), `target`, `arrival` (point on the target), `facing`, `side`, `kind` (`door`, `front_door`, `secret_exit`) |
| `spawn` | default standing point |
| `item_slot` | where the room's carryable item (or the glass) sits (rooms and secret passage) |
| `guest_spots` | at least 2 points where a guest can stand; staring happens at these |

Top level: `start`, `supports_suspects` (a layout's room count must equal `ROOM_COUNT` for each listed suspect
count), `px_per_step` (assumption, 24), and `secret` (`screen`, `entrance_room`, `exit_room`, `panel_wall`,
seven `panel_columns`, as observed in the original).

`problems(layout)` checks: rectangles on screen, spawn/item/guest spots on open floor, portals at the edge of
floor and not inside obstacles, arrivals on open floor of the target, every `door` has a door back, every
screen reachable from the start (the secret panel counts as an edge), room counts match the generator.

## Furniture kinds and guest spots (labels, 2026-10-26)

Two optional label lists run in parallel with a screen's lists and must have the same length:

- `obstacle_kinds`: one string per obstacle: `bookshelf`, `table`, `bed`, `window`, `sofa`, `crate`, and so on. Rough
  on purpose; furniture ids and seat spots are added later.
- `guest_spot_kinds`: one string per guest spot: `stand`, `seat`, `table`, `browse`, `window`, `fireplace`,
  `stairs`. The `stand` kind is used when nothing is nearby.
- `guest_spot_facing`: one value per guest spot: `"north"`, `"east"`, `"south"` or `"west"`, `"any"`, or a list
  of 2-3 sides in north/east/south/west order such as `["north", "east"]`. It says which ways a guest may face
  when standing there. Rotating a room rotates every side.

**These fields describe the spot, not who stands on it** (user, 2026-10-26). They are completely separate from
personalities. Personalities stay voice and mood only for the case data; later a personality will *use* a spot's
allowed facings and kinds when it picks a stance or pose, but nothing about a personality is stored in the map.

## House 01 (first blockout)

One floor with two **large halls** (user, 2026-10-26: corridors with 4-6 doors, like the original's long
corridors, and some north-south). The **main hall** runs east-west and has five room doors (library, study,
ballroom to the north; kitchen, parlor to the south) and the locked front door at its west end. The **east hall**
is a tall, narrow north-south corridor (`orientation: "ns"`) with four doors, two on each side wall: bedroom and
dining room to the west, bathroom and sewing room to the east. Rooms off it open on their side walls instead of
north or south. The main hall's east end meets the east hall's north end. The secret passage is entered from a
panel on the library's north wall and exits one-way into the sewing room. 9 ordinary rooms + the secret
passage = 10 rooms, the size a six-suspect case needs. Step cost between rooms at the assumed 24 px per step:
13 for neighbours to 44 for the far ends (median 31); the original needed about 19 from one room to the next on a
tour. Placeholder numbers, to be tuned in the viewer.

Hall screens carry `orientation` (`ew` or `ns`) so a viewer knows which way the corridor runs.
